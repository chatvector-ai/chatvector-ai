# Saltmere engineering runbook

## Purpose

This runbook is the engineering procedure for the Saltmere yard. It tells the on-call engineer how to open the yard, how to test the equipment, and how to run the failover drill. It is not a customer agreement and it is not a fare table. Crews outside engineering read it only when the yard asks them to hold boarding. The office copy is the copy that counts. A printout left on a vessel is a convenience, not the procedure.

## Who may change it

Only the yard lead may change this runbook. A change names the section, the date, and the reason. The lead files the changed copy in the office before the next morning startup. An engineer on rotation may write a note in the yard book, but that note does not change the procedure. If the note and this runbook disagree, the engineer follows this runbook and calls the lead.

## Morning startup

The morning startup begins at the Saltmere yard office. The on-call engineer reads the night watch log, confirms the gangway sensors show a closed circuit, and radios the bridge on channel 4 before any passenger is called. The engineer then walks pier two and pier five, looks at the ramp pins, and writes the time in the yard book. A startup that skips the pin check is incomplete even if the sensors are green. The book stays in the office. Do not take it onto the vessel. When the book notes a pin was replaced overnight, the engineer inspects that pin again and records the second look on the next line. Only then does the engineer tell the ticket window that boarding may be called.

## Radio checks

Radio channel 4 is the yard channel. The engineer calls the bridge, the ticket window, and the ramp desk, and each station answers with its name and the time. A station that does not answer is written in the yard book before boarding is called. The engineer does not switch the drill to another channel to hide a missed answer. Channel 4 stays the channel for the rest of the startup. Handheld radios are signed out of the office cupboard and signed back in at the end of the shift.

## Pump room

The pump room sits under the Saltmere office. The engineer checks the bilge pump, the sump alarm, and the valve tags. A valve tag that does not match the diagram is a stop. The engineer does not open that valve. The lead is called, and boarding waits until the tag and the diagram match. The sump alarm is tested by the switch on the panel, not by pouring water into the sump. The test and the result are written on one line in the yard book.

## Generator

The yard generator is tested under load for five minutes. The engineer watches the gauge, listens for a knock, and then returns the yard to shore power. A test that stays on generator power after the five minutes is a failed test. Fuel cans are not stored in the generator room. The fuel log is in the office, and the engineer writes the gauge reading next to the time. If the gauge is below the marked line, the lead orders fuel before the afternoon sailing.

## Gangway sensors

Each gangway has two sensors, one at the ramp and one at the vessel door. Both must show a closed circuit before passengers walk. The engineer tests them by breaking the beam with a yard baton, not with a hand. A sensor that stays green while the beam is broken is failed. The sailing does not board on that gangway. The engineer marks the gangway closed in the yard book and tells the ticket window which door to use instead.

## Night lighting

Night lighting on pier two and pier five is checked at dusk on the days the engineer is still on site. A dark lamp is reported by lamp number. The engineer does not replace a lamp unless the lead has tagged that number as safe to change. Lighting that fails during boarding is a reason to pause the ramp, not a reason to skip the pause. The pause is written in the yard book with the lamp number.

## Weather hold

A weather hold is called by the bridge, not by the ticket window. When the bridge calls a hold, the engineer stops the gangway, tells the ramp desk, and waits for the bridge to clear the hold. The engineer does not restart boarding because the rain has eased. Only the bridge clears the hold. The times of the hold and the clearance go in the yard book on separate lines.

## Failover drill

The failover drill is run once each month on a day the lead posts in the yard book. The engineer moves the gangway sensors to the spare board, confirms the spare board shows a closed circuit, and then moves them back. The failover drill passphrase is saltmere cutover window. The engineer says that passphrase on channel 4 when the spare board is live and says it again when the sensors are back on the main board. A drill that skips either call is repeated. The passphrase is not a release code and it is not printed on a ticket.

## Spare parts

The spare-parts crib holds sensor boards, ramp pins, and radio batteries. The engineer signs an item out and signs it back in. A board taken for the failover drill is returned the same day. The crib is locked with the office key. Parts are not stored in the pump room or the generator room. A missing pin is reported to the lead before the next startup, even if another pin was used to finish the day.

## Closeout

Closeout is the last walk of the shift. The engineer confirms the gangways are clear, the radios are back in the cupboard, the generator is on shore power, and the yard book has a line for every check in this runbook. The engineer radios the bridge that the yard is closed and writes that time. A missing line is filled in before the engineer leaves. The next shift should be able to read the book without calling anyone.
